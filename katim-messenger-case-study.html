The Problem

The existing messenger had shipped years earlier, designed for a single device and a single visual language. As KATIM expanded to gov&defense clients across iOS, Android, Web, and its own hardware, the app's navigation, iconography, and information architecture hadn't kept pace — and there was still no light theme, no dark theme discipline, and no shared design system to keep three engineering teams in sync.

High impactTab bar navigation icons were too small; inactive icons looked disabled, and the icon competed visually with its own notification bubble.
Medium impactIcons and their supporting status text weren't in close proximity, creating ambiguity and cluttering the layout (e.g. the outgoing-call icon and its label).
Low impactIconography was inconsistent — filled and outline styles were mixed throughout the same screens.

Key Decisions
01

Collapse "Timeline" into clear Messages & Calls

Timeline (mixed feed)→MessagesCalls

The existing IA buried both conversations and call history inside a single ambiguous "Timeline" concept. Splitting it into two unambiguous entry points removed a full click for every user, on every session, for the app's two most frequent actions.

02

Let calling start from inside a conversation

Users previously had to back out of a chat to place a call. We moved voice/video initiation directly onto the message details screen, so a conversation could continue as a call without switching context or losing the thread.

03

Ask "group or individual" before search, not after

Search all contacts→Select multiple→Confirm group?
Group or 1:1?→Search/select

Since most conversations are 1:1, forcing every user through a multi-select flow by default added unnecessary steps. Asking for chat type upfront and branching the search experience accordingly cut roughly two clicks from the most common "start a new chat" path.

Lessons Learned
Dual ownership builds range
Balancing Program Director and Lead UX Designer roles taught me how design quality and program execution constantly influence each other, not separate tracks.
Governance is a design skill too
Structuring sprints, resourcing, and project governance felt as critical to the outcome as the design decisions themselves.
Distance demands documentation discipline
Working with a globally distributed dev team with no direct channel taught me that clear, structured documentation isn't a nice-to-have — it's the primary interface when direct collaboration isn't possible.
Complexity rewards resourcefulness over rigidity
Ambiguity and multi-hat responsibility were manageable by staying adaptable rather than over-relying on a fixed process.
