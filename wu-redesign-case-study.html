The Problem

WU ran five separate apps for the same user. Each had its own stack, team, and experience. The business model shift to account-based required one platform — not five.

My Role
Design Strategy & Direction
Set the end-to-end UX vision for E1, translating business objectives into a coherent design direction across all platforms and markets.
Investment Case for Nexus DS
Made the case to CPO and Engineering leadership for a dedicated design system effort before a single production screen was designed.
Dual-Track Agile Process Design
Introduced and embedded dual-track agile across squads — keeping discovery one sprint ahead of delivery to prevent engineering bottlenecks.
Epic Sequencing & RICE Prioritisation
Collaborated with Product to build the delivery roadmap, sequencing design dependencies as first-class constraints.
Stakeholder Alignment
Held alignment across CPO, Engineering VPs, Compliance, and Brand — negotiating scope trade-offs while protecting core UX outcomes.
Design QA Rituals
Created and enforced a pre-launch UX review ritual — a quality gate between design approval and engineering shipment that reduced rework by 40%.
Team Leadership — 11 Designers
Led weekly 1:1s, critique sessions, and career development conversations across a multi-disciplinary team embedded in four agile squads.
Key Decisions
01

Design system before production screens

Six weeks in, engineering flagged that building a design system in parallel with production screens was unsustainable. I pushed back on delaying, reducing, or dropping the system — made the case to the CPO that a half-built system would cost more in rework than a short delay. We got three additional weeks. The Nexus Design System shipped before production. That decision is why 142 screens shipped at consistent quality.

02

Dual-track agile to unblock engineering

Before
Design → Idle → Eng
After
Discovery sprint
Delivery sprint → Eng

Framing it as an engineering throughput improvement, not a design quality argument, got PM and Engineering buy-in in two weeks. Design QA rituals introduced alongside cut rework by 40%.

03

Person-first IA over corridor-first

Old — 7 steps
1Select destination country
2Enter recipient details
3Enter amount
+ 4 more steps
New — 3 steps
1Choose recipient
2Enter amount + see rates
3Confirm & send

Card sorting with real users confirmed people think about the recipient first, not the corridor. This reordering restructured the entire app’s entry point and reduced the perceived journey from 7 steps to 3.

Design Style

Four design directions were presented to leadership, each resolving the same core screen with a different visual language. Direction 01 received the most votes and survived a second round of critique before becoming E1’s visual foundation.

Direction 01 — Selected
Scannable Minimalistic High contrast WCAG 2.1 AA Subtle burst of colour
Balanced WU’s brand warmth with a modern, account-based product language. High contrast ratios met accessibility compliance across all markets. Clear visual hierarchy reduced cognitive load for low-literacy users — enough visual elevation to compete with challenger fintech products without abandoning the trust signals WU users relied on.
UX Review Ritual

A quality gate between “design approved” and “engineering shipped” — not a visual QA pass, but a systematic review of the complete application against original experience intent.
